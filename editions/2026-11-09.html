The big story

Budget 2026 is built around "economic sovereignty"

Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne tabled Budget 2026, titled Masters of our Destiny, on Thursday. It proposes $142.1 billion in new measures, mostly offset by higher-than-expected federal revenue, and frames much of the spending around reducing Canada's reliance on the United States. It is the first budget since the Liberals won a majority, so it can pass without help from other parties. Most sections below have a budget angle.

Sources: Narcity / Canadian Press · Doane Grant Thornton

Ottawa

A federal plan to end homelessness, and how the mayor sees it

The budget promises to end homelessness in Canada within ten years. It sets aside $11.2 billion over four years starting next year, and $17.8 billion over ten years. Mayor Mark Sutcliffe said much of the budget lines up with the city's priorities, including his goal of ending youth homelessness in Ottawa by 2030. He said he wasn't worried that the budget had no new money for public transit.

Sources: CTV News · CTV News Ottawa

The North

Budget renews the Inuit Child First Initiative, under Inuit control

The budget commits $575 million over five years, starting in 2027-28, to renew the Inuit Child First Initiative. The program will move to Pirutsiagutiit, an Inuit-designed program developed with Inuit Tapiriit Kanatami (ITK) and the four Inuit treaty organizations. It is set to start April 1, 2027, and will be run by the treaty organizations. ITK had asked for $600 million.

Nutrition North gets $610.3 million over four years, also starting in 2027-28. ITK welcomed the child program funding. It also said the budget keeps Nutrition North's inefficiencies and has no Inuit-specific housing or infrastructure money, compared with $30 billion for First Nations. The Conservative critic called the Nutrition North money a stopgap.

Source: Nunatsiaq News (Nov. 6)

Nunavut MLAs wrap up the fall sitting

The legislature passed seven of the eleven bills it considered over two weeks. They include doubling a student loan limit to $300,000 and the capital budget for 2027-28. Four bills were held over. MLAs return February 27.

Source: Nunatsiaq News (Nov. 6)

Tech, policy and sovereignty

The budget swaps "make the platforms pay" for public money

Michael Geist argues the budget largely drops the strategy of making large tech platforms fund Canadian news and culture through the Online News Act and Online Streaming Act. Instead, taxpayers would provide roughly $1.5 billion in new or renewed support. That includes one year of streaming money, journalism tax credits extended to broadcasters, and a permanent expansion of the Local Journalism Initiative. Separately, the budget's $600 million for Canadian content covers 2026-27 only and replaces streamer contributions the CRTC had required.

Sources: Michael Geist (Nov. 6) · CP24 / Canadian Press

A new digital safety regulator gets its funding

The budget sets aside $176.3 million over five years for a new Digital Safety and Data Protection Commission. It would enforce the proposed online safety rules, including the under-16 social media restriction in Bill C-34, as well as private-sector privacy law. C-34 has just finished three days of House debate before committee hearings. Geist's read of that debate is critical.

Sources: CP24 / Canadian Press · Michael Geist (Nov. 5)

The lawful access bill could reach gaming platforms and AI services

At Senate hearings on Bill C-22, officials acknowledged its wording is broader than comparable laws in other countries. Geist argues the bill is built to reach a wide range of services, including gaming platforms with chat and AI services.

Source: Michael Geist (Nov. 4)

AI across the public service

The budget includes about $1 billion over five years for a Government Transformation Fund to speed up federal IT projects. Digital Transformation Canada will lead AI adoption across the public service. The Public Service Alliance of Canada says the rollout lacks real privacy protections.

Source: CP24 / Canadian Press
